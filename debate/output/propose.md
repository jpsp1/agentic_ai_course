I strongly support the motion that **LLMs should be strongly regulated by law**.

Why? Because LLMs are not ordinary tools. They can generate persuasive misinformation at scale, enable fraud and scams, leak sensitive data, and amplify harmful bias—all with unprecedented speed and reach. Left unchecked, the incentives of profit and competition push companies to release more powerful systems before they are fully safe.

Strong regulation is not anti-innovation; it is what makes innovation trustworthy. We regulate cars, medicine, aviation, and finance because when a technology can cause large-scale harm, society does not wait for disaster before acting. LLMs are already influencing elections, education, hiring, customer service, and security. That means the risks are no longer theoretical.

Law should require:
- transparency about training data and system limitations,
- independent safety testing before deployment,
- accountability for harmful outputs and misuse,
- protections for privacy and copyright,
- and clear rules against deception, discrimination, and unsafe high-risk uses.

Without legal standards, the public bears the costs while companies capture the benefits. Strong regulation creates a baseline of safety, responsibility, and trust. If we want LLMs to serve society instead of destabilizing it, regulation is not optional—it is essential.
